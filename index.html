Sri Lankan elephant

The largest of the Asian elephant subspecies, found only on one island. It shapes the forests it lives in and the festivals people hold.

How big is big?

A large bull stands roughly twice as tall as an adult person at the shoulder.

Life in the wild

Diet

Grass makes up most of the diet, with bark, leaves, roots and fruit. An adult can eat around 150 kg a day and feeds for up to 16 hours.

Water

Elephants drink 100 to 200 litres a day, so they stay close to rivers, tanks and lakes. They bathe and spray mud to protect their skin.

Herd life

Herds of related females and calves are led by an older female, the matriarch. Adult males often roam alone or in small groups.

Conservation

The species is listed as endangered. Habitat loss and conflict with farmers are the main threats. A 2011 census counted about 5,900 elephants.

What sets it apart

  • Most males have no tusks. Males with tusks, called tuskers, are rare in Sri Lanka.
  • Pale pink patches often show on the ears, face and trunk.
  • The forehead has two rounded domes with a dip between them.
  • A newborn calf weighs about 100 kg.
  • Large gatherings form at Minneriya National Park in the dry season.

Try a random fact

Elephants use their trunks to breathe, smell, drink, and pick up food.
